Epoxy Floor Maintenance Tips

Your epoxy floor is a long-term investment — with the right care routine, it can look showroom-fresh for 15 to 20 years. Here are the exact steps our team uses and recommends to keep every coating performing at its best.

Daily Habits That Make a Big Difference

The simplest thing you can do is sweep or dust-mop the floor every day. Grit, sand, and fine debris act like sandpaper underfoot, slowly dulling the surface over time. A microfiber dust mop is ideal because it picks up particles without scratching.

Spill response matters. Epoxy is resistant, but not invincible. Wipe up motor oil, gasoline, antifreeze, and harsh chemicals within minutes — not hours. Use a clean rag and a mild degreaser for oily residues.

Weekly Deep-Clean Routine

Once a week, mop the floor using warm water mixed with a pH-neutral cleaner (we recommend Simple Green diluted 1:10). Here's the process that works:

  1. Sweep or vacuum the entire surface first.
  2. Mop with the diluted solution using a soft microfiber mop.
  3. Rinse with clean water to avoid soap buildup.
  4. Squeegee or air-dry — never leave standing water.

What to avoid at all costs: citrus-based or vinegar cleaners (they degrade epoxy over time), steel wool, abrasive scouring pads, and any soap that leaves a sudsy film. These will create a hazy, slippery residue that's hard to remove.

Monthly and Seasonal Care

Protect Against Hot Tire Pickup

If you park vehicles on an epoxy garage floor, allow tires to cool for 30 minutes before pulling in. Hot tires from highway driving can temporarily soften standard epoxy and leave marks. Polyaspartic topcoats dramatically reduce this risk — ask us about upgrading your finish.

Use Floor Mats Strategically

Place rubber-backed mats at entry points to trap grit before it reaches your floor. Under heavy equipment, jack stands, and toolboxes, use rubber pads to prevent point-load indentations.

Annual Inspection

Once a year, inspect the floor under good lighting for any chips, peeling edges, or areas where the topcoat looks thin. Small chips caught early can be spot-repaired for a fraction of the cost of a full re-coat.

Products We Trust — and Products to Avoid

  • ✅ Safe cleaners: Simple Green, Zep Neutral Floor Cleaner, diluted dish soap (rinse well)
  • ✅ Safe tools: Microfiber mop, soft-bristle broom, rubber squeegee
  • ❌ Avoid: Bleach at full strength, muriatic acid, pine-based cleaners, Comet or Ajax powder
  • ❌ Avoid: Pressure washing with high heat or steam — it can break the bond of the topcoat

When to Call a Professional

If you notice widespread yellowing (common with standard epoxy exposed to UV), large areas of peeling, or persistent stains that won't lift, it's time for a professional assessment. A re-coat or polyaspartic topcoat application can restore the floor to like-new condition without a full tearout.
